## B. Market context

### B1. Royalty Pharma and others on Chinese-originated royalties (2025–2026)

- **Investor Day, Sep 2025.** The slide "China emerging as a significant driver for innovation" counts royalties created from China out-licensing at 2 (2019) to 3–4 (2015–17), 9, 25 and then 43 (2024).
  - The slide also says: "Royalty market will take time to develop as activity has been focused on therapies in early-stage development"; "Capital markets less developed in China, creating more acute need for alternative sources of capital"; "RP is focused on developing relationships with Chinese biopharma companies" ([deck](https://www.royaltypharma.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Investor-Day-Consolidated-Slides-vFinal.pdf)).
- **Q3 2025 call (Nov 5, 2025), Chris Hite.** "A lot of those deals out of China have been early-stage development deals. We're going to track those transactions and those molecules as they progress… there certainly will be opportunities to acquire those royalties… We've had multiple teams go to China this year" ([transcript](https://www.royaltypharma.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/RPRX-Q3_2025_Transcript.pdf)).
- **10-K FY2025.** "The royalty market in China represents an important long-term opportunity as the licensing activity has primarily been focused on therapies in early-stage development." Imdelltra brought in $10M of portfolio receipts in 2025 ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1802768/000180276826000003/rprx-20251231.htm)).
- **Mar 2, 2026.** RP hired Kenneth Sun (ex-Morgan Stanley Head of APAC Healthcare IB) as SVP and Head of Asia, based in Hong Kong, effective May 2026.
  - The release cites Chinese out-licensing at more than $130B of announced value in 2025, against about $14B in 2021.
  - Sun is quoted: "Royalty creation in Asia has grown rapidly and represents a potentially important new source of flexible, non-dilutive capital" ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/02/3247202/0/en/royalty-pharma-appoints-kenneth-sun-as-senior-vice-president-and-head-of-asia-to-expand-royalty-pharma-s-global-platform.html)).
  - The Hong Kong office at IFC opened in May 2026 ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3358830/funding-option-royalties-giant-opens-hong-kong-base-mainland-china-biotech-deals-surge); [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/103249472.html)).
- **Q2 2026 call (Aug 2026), Legorreta.**
  - "The IP is put into an offshore entity" and contracts are governed by US or European law.
  - "There is a substantial number of royalty agreements already in place."
  - RP is monitoring the **COINS Act and BINSA** ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/royalty-pharma-rprx-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).
- **Morgan Stanley Healthcare Conference, Sep 14, 2026.**
  - RP has identified about 3,000 Chinese biotechs and sees China as "a major long-term opportunity" that "will take time to scale."
  - It says Chinese royalty rates run "in the teens to 20s," against 2–7% for US academic royalties.
  - It sees less political sensitivity in royalties than in equity, because IP sits in Cayman/Jersey subsidiaries and the "cash flows and credit risk remain with U.S. or European pharmaceutical partners."
  - Its only completed China deal so far is BeOne/Imdelltra ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/royalty-pharma-at-morgan-stanley-conference-growth-broadens-beyond-royalties-93CH-4900262)).
- **CBC/R-Bridge (Fund II, $500M, Oct 2025).** It has become "more selective, preferring approved products in major markets over higher-risk clinical-stage Chinese assets" ([ION Analytics](https://ionanalytics.com/insights/mergermarket/cbc-group-closes-second-healthcare-private-credit-fund-expands-remit-beyond-china/)).
- **Counter-trend in royalty rates.** Evaluate data via BioSpace (Jun 3, 2026) says average royalty floors in China deals fell from 7.1% to 5.5% in Jan–Apr 2026. Some licensors accept floors of 2–3% in exchange for larger upfronts, which average $167M, up 36% ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/business/no-longer-a-bargain-pool-chinese-biotechs-command-higher-premiums)). That means newer deals carry thinner royalties, and 2023–2025 vintages may be the richer screening pool. RP's "teens to 20s" figure may describe selected deals; reconcile the two per contract.

### B3. Regulatory and legal friction for Chinese sellers

**US side**
- **BIOSECURE Act.** Enacted in the FY2026 NDAA, signed **2025-12-18**.
  - It restricts federal contracting and grants involving "biotechnology companies of concern." Designation runs through an OMB process linked to the DoD 1260H list.
  - OMB guidance was due about June 2026. The **initial BCC list is due by 2026-12-18**. FAR amendments are expected around 2027, and prohibitions take effect after that.
  - It does not directly bar private royalty purchases ([Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/biosecure-act-becomes-law); [Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/01/united-states-the-biosecure-act-becomes-law); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/alerts-otherindustries-final-fy-2026-ndaa-includes-biosecure)).
  - Morgan Lewis notes that DoD recently added "several prominent biotech firms" to the 1260H list, which creates a "halo effect" on deal appetite ([Morgan Lewis, Jun 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/us-china-biotech-transactions-navigating-each-countrys-evolving-regulatory-landscape)).
- **COINS Act (outbound investment).** Enacted Dec 2025. It covers AI, semiconductors and quantum. **Biotech is not yet covered**; Treasury may add sectors by **2027-03-13** ([Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/china-biotech-deals-are-in-the-u-s-congress-crosshairs-heres-what-life-scienc-102n1aq); Morgan Lewis).
- **BINSA (Biotech Investment National Security Act).**
  - House bill from Moolenaar and Dingell, introduced June 2026; Senate companion from Slotkin and Ricketts on 2026-08-06.
  - It would add biotech to COINS and make licensing ("licensing of a prohibited technology from a covered foreign person"), JVs and equity deals with Chinese covered persons subject to Treasury review. Regulations would follow within a year.
  - As currently drafted it applies **prospectively only**. It is still a bill, not law ([Fierce](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/divisive-bill-seeks-add-biotech-industry-law-limiting-investment-chinese-industry); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/china-biotech-deals-are-in-the-u-s-congress-crosshairs-heres-what-life-scienc-102n1aq)).
  - None of the sources reviewed addresses whether a US buyer purchasing an *existing* royalty receivable from a Chinese licensor would be covered. **Open question.**
- **Draft executive order (Sep 2025, reported by the NYT).** It would have routed US–China licensing deals through CFIUS, increased FDA scrutiny of China-generated data and added fees. The White House said it was "not actively considering" it. There was no formal action as of the most recent reporting found (Jan 2026) ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/policy/trump-reportedly-preparing-order-restricting-chinese-drug-licensing-deals); [Fierce](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/trump-admin-mulls-severe-restrictions-us-pharmas-securing-chinese-meds-nyt)).
  - House Appropriations report language (non-binding) directs FDA not to accept data from Chinese sites (Morgan Lewis).
- **CFIUS.** Applies to *inbound* investment; it is relevant for sensitive data or genomic assets (Morgan Lewis). My analysis, not sourced: a US royalty buyer purchasing a receivable from a Chinese seller is not an inbound investment.

**China side**
- **Outbound Investment Regulations (State Council Decree No. 837).** Effective **2026-07-01**.
  - Pure cash license-outs "generally remain outside the scope" and are encouraged as FX-earning technology export.
  - **Equity consideration (NewCo stakes), JVs and IP transfers increasingly trigger security review.** Look-through rules target structures parked in Cayman, BVI or Hong Kong (Morgan Lewis, Jun 2026).
  - This bears on NewCo equity secondaries (A3).
- **Technology export approvals or registration and ODI filings** apply to NewCo deals ([Morgan Lewis, Apr 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/04/a-strategic-playbook-for-chinese-biotech-cross-border-deals-navigating-the-new-value-paradigm)).
  - The amended Foreign Trade Law (effective 2026-03-01) adds liability for unauthorized export of restricted technology.
- **SAFE/FX.** Cross-border licensing payments into and out of China go through bank-verified SAFE documentation and tax registration with withholding ([Acclime](https://china.acclime.com/guides/ensuring-safe-payment-into-and-out-of-china/)).
  - I found **no source on SAFE treatment of an onshore licensor selling its royalty receivable offshore**. Expect contract re-registration and FX-receipt documentation questions.
  - RP's stated workaround is that most Chinese out-licensed IP sits in offshore entities, so the receivable can be sold offshore. Check each candidate's licensor entity (onshore PRC vs Cayman/HK).
- **Offshore structures.** The CSRC now scrutinizes offshore vehicles more closely for listings ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/business/banking-finance/article/3349166/stricter-chinese-scrutiny-offshore-vehicles-blow-tech-and-biotech-ipo-candidates)).

### B4. Osagie Imasogie / PIPV Capital: thesis summary

- **Who.** Osagie Imasogie is co-founder and Senior Managing Partner of PIPV Capital (Phoenix IP Ventures), described as "an IP-based Merchant Bank." Earlier he conceived and ran **GlaxoSmithKline Ventures**, GSK's "intellectual property-based venture capital fund." There he and his team placed GSK's **non-progressed R&D assets** into new or existing companies, in about 30 deals from 2000 to 2003.
  - He teaches "Intellectual Property and National Economic Value Creation" at Penn Carey Law.
  - Sources: [Penn Carey Law](https://www.law.upenn.edu/faculty/oimasogi); [Quoin](https://quoinadvisors.com/osagie-imasogie/).
- **Thesis, as stated in the Ibere Pharmaceuticals SPAC S-1 (2021).** PIPV is "a healthcare sector-focused private equity manager specializing in acquiring revenue-generating, high quality assets in the pharmaceutical and life sciences industries." It treats IP-protected drug assets (mature brands, reformulation platforms, out-licensed or non-core pipeline) as an investable, cash-flowing asset class.
  - Claimed track record: more than $1B of institutional capital deployed and "returns exceeding 20%."
  - Examples: **Iroko** (bought mature brands such as Aggrastat, Indocin and Vancocin, with gross margins above 50%); **iCeutica** (SoluMatrix reformulations Zorvolex and Vivlodex, with royalty structures); **Ception** (sold to Cephalon for $350M, plus about $200M of approval milestones) ([SEC S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1835205/000110465921015548/tm215210d1_s-1.htm)).
  - I found no PIPV statement specific to China or Asia.
